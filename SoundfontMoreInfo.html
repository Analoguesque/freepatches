Bank 001 - Cheezoids
This is the ultimate Digital emulation SoundFont Bank, and follows a path from 1981 to 1984, when analog was dying and the dawn of cheap digital had arrived. Did you miss out on the pioneering years of synth music? Did you miss out on Hi-NRG, Eurobeat, or Italo Disco? Do you remember Trans-X, Tapps, The Flirts, People Like Us, Lime, or Fancy? Do you remember Speak and Spell from Depeche Mode, Yazoo (Yaz), Blancmange, OMD, and Dare from The Human League? Well this is your chance to get to grips with the type of sounds they were using during those pioneering synth years. A solid 128 patches of the coolest retro digital sounds around, and all with the usual Analoguesque quality. This SoundFont is predominantly Pads and Leads, but still with enough rocking bass thrown in to keep it mixed.

Bank 002 - Digital Bliss
Digital Bliss follows in the very ample footsteps of Vintage Synth 2001, but this time with an emphasis on vintage digital and hybrid synths rather than just analog. However, this is not to say that all the sounds are digital. It contains a full complement of 128 patches, concentrating on digital pads, leads, basses, and FX, but still with some analog patches thrown in for good measure because you can't get enough of those! Don't mistake digital to mean thin. This SoundFont has enough low-end ballz to knock an elephant on its back! Several velocity-sensitive patches are also included, each responding to up to 7 layers of velocity. This gives you extremely expressive playability, which brings a live feel to your music and allows playback just like old analog synths.

Bank 003 - Vintage Drums 2001
Being the sister of Vintage Synth 2001, Vintage Drums 2001 will keep your ears tuned in for months! Obligatory TR909, TR808, and CR-78 emulations are included, as well as many other drum machines, real and imagined! Remember that since these are designed from the ground up rather than sampled from the original instruments, you do not have anything like this in your collection. They are very unique sounds with a flavour of the original drumboxes. Some TR909 kicks are heavier than the original TR909. Some TR808 snares are snappier than the original TR808. In all cases, you get something unique and you get a lot of it. Favorites include the two huge analog kits, with around 6 octaves of percussion each, as well as the clean-sounding TR and CR kits, and the amazingly versatile FM Percussion kit! The list is extensive and includes over 500 drum sounds! A lot of people have raved about the kits in this bank and at this price you are getting a serious bargain. Vintage Drums 2001 is an absolutely massive SoundFont with a wealth of phatt, thumping, grungy, and cheesy sounds to satisfy even the most ardent vintage synth fan. There are a lot of sounds to choose from in this bank!

Bank 005 - Vintage Dreams Waves 2.1
This freeware SoundFont Bank from one of the principal Analoguesque designers has been around for several years already (1996), and is still (along with Vintage Dreams Drums) consistently one of the most downloaded on the web. At only 117Kb uncompressed, it contains a full 128 presets in the instrument section. The 8 drum kits in the percussion section have been removed and can be found separately as Vintage Dreams Drums. Although now hopelessly outdated and showing it's age, this SoundFont Bank has been compared to 4Mb, 8Mb, and even 16Mb SoundFont Banks by some users! I'm not sure we would go so far as to compare it to 16Mb, but it certainly is worth a lot more than it's 117Kb would suggest. Is it worth the download? Sure. Is it anything like the commercial SoundFont Banks? No, the quality is nowhere near our commercial SoundFont Banks, although the principles are very similar.

Bank 006 - Vintage Dreams Drums
This SoundFont Bank is simply the drum kit presets from the original Vintage Dreams Waves 2.0, which were removed f or version 2.1 above. They are no longer available in Vintage Dreams Waves 2.1. The drum kits have been inserted into both the percussion and instrument preset sections, which allows their use on all midi channels. You can use hi-hats from one kit, combined with a snare from another kit, and a kick from yet another kit - You simply assign different kits to different midi channels. This SoundFont Bank is also showing it's age and cannot compare to it's newer siblings, but it is still quite useful in many situations, such as trance, electronica ... etc.
